What is YOLOv8?

Single-Forward-Pass Deep Neural Object Detection

YOLO stands for "You Only Look Once". Unlike traditional computer algorithms that scan an image piece-by-piece, YOLOv8 passes the whole picture through a deep neural network once in milliseconds. It spots objects (bottles, cans, paper) and draws tight coordinate bounding boxes around them instantly.

Inference Latency < 15 ms
Architecture CSP-Darknet + SPPF
Resolution 640×640 Tensor
Technical Specification & Architecture:
  • Anchor-Free Detection: Eliminates predefined anchor boxes, predicting object centers directly for higher accuracy on squished and deformed waste.
  • Edge Optimization: Exported to ONNX and OpenVINO formats for zero-lag CPU and NPU acceleration without discrete GPU requirement.
  • Confidence Threshold: Configured at 0.50 minimum intersection-over-union (IoU) to prevent false-positive contamination alerts.

What are Resin Codes (RIC #1–#7)?

Polymer Chemistry vs. Municipal Collectibility

Molded into almost every commercial plastic package is a triangle arrow symbol with a number from 1 to 7. #1 (PET) and #2 (HDPE) are the most valuable and easiest to melt into new products. #3 (PVC) and #6 (Styrofoam) rarely belong in household curbside bins because they degrade or release chemicals when melted.

Codification ASTM D7611 / ISO
High-Value Resins #1 (PET) & #2 (HDPE)
Curbside Hazard #3 (PVC) & #6 (PS)
Technical Specification & Architecture:
  • Density Float-Sink Separation: Industrial MRFs separate shredded plastics by density: PET (1.38 g/cm³) sinks in water tanks, while HDPE (0.95 g/cm³) and PP (0.90 g/cm³) float.
  • Cross-Polymer Degradation: If PVC (1.40 g/cm³) mixes into PET, it decomposes at PET processing temperatures (260°C), generating corrosive hydrochloric acid that destroys equipment.
  • Secondary rPET Market: Mechanically washed clear flake sells for $450–$620/ton to produce food-grade packaging and polyester fibers.

Why Does Bin Contamination Matter?

Protecting Commodity Bale Purity from Landfill Diversion

If someone tosses greasy takeout boxes or unrinsed milk jugs into a recycling bin, food fats and residual liquids soak into clean paper and cardboard fibers. This "contamination" ruins recycling batches, causing entire truckloads to be diverted to landfills or incinerators.

Max Tolerance < 5% Contamination
Failure Mode Fiber Oil Saturation
Consequence 10-Ton Bale Rejection
Technical Specification & Architecture:
  • The Grease Problem: Paper recycling uses water slurry baths. Oil and grease do not dissolve in water; they bond to paper fibers, creating oily spots that weaken cardboard structural integrity.
  • Optical Sorter Confusion: Greasy or crushed multi-layer cartons fool Near-Infrared (NIR) optical sensors, causing misdirection into the wrong processing streams.
  • The Economic Impact: A single contaminated MRF bale costs municipal sorting facilities $2,000+ in transport fines and landfill tipping fees.
